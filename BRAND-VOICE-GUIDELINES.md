- A trusted colleague who happens to have deep expertise in learning science
- The department's go-to person for pedagogical questions

## Tone Variations by Context

### 1. Educational Content (Blog, Guides, Tooltips)
**Tone:** Collegial Expert
- Use first-person plural ("we") to include the reader
- Cite research naturally, not defensively
- Offer perspective, not prescriptions

**Example:**
> "Cognitive load theory suggests that learners have limited working memory capacity. When we sequence information strategically—building from foundational concepts to complex applications—we create cognitive space for deeper processing."

### 2. Product Interface (UI Copy, Buttons, Labels)
**Tone:** Clear & Confident
- Minimal words, maximum clarity
- Action-oriented but not pushy
- Professional terminology where appropriate

**Examples:**
- Button: "Generate Objectives" (not "Create Now!")
- Label: "Bloom's Taxonomy Alignment" (not "Level Checker")
- Status: "Analysis complete" (not "Done! 🎉")

### 3. Onboarding & Help
**Tone:** Supportive Guide
- Anticipate questions
- Acknowledge the instructor's expertise
- Frame features as tools, not solutions

**Example:**
> "You know your students best. The Adaptive Pathways tool helps you map that knowledge into personalized learning sequences—offering a starting point you can refine."

### 4. Error Messages & System Feedback
**Tone:** Honest & Helpful
- Acknowledge the issue directly
- Explain what happened in plain terms
- Offer a clear path forward

**Example:**
> "We couldn't process the uploaded syllabus. The file may be corrupted or in an unsupported format. Try uploading a PDF, DOCX, or plain text file."

### 5. Marketing & Communications
**Tone:** Quietly Confident
- Lead with value, not features
- Appeal to professional identity
- Avoid hyperbole and buzzwords

**Example:**
> "Course design shaped by research. Lecture preparation informed by evidence. Assessment aligned with outcomes. Teaching Assistant brings the insights of learning science into your daily practice."

## Language Guidelines

### Preferred Vocabulary

| Instead of... | Use... | Why |
|---------------|--------|-----|
| Cutting-edge, revolutionary | Evidence-based, research-informed | Substance over hype |
| Users | Instructors, educators, faculty | Respect professional identity |
| Content | Learning materials, course resources | Educational terminology |
| AI-powered | Intelligent assistance, AI-assisted | Focus on outcome, not technology |
| Easy, simple | Streamlined, efficient | Respect complexity of teaching |
| Best practices | Effective approaches, research-supported strategies | Avoid platitudes |
| Leverage, utilize | Use, apply | Plain language |
| Learnings | Insights, findings | Professional usage |
| Solution | Tool, approach, method | Less corporate |
| Transform | Enhance, refine, strengthen | More measured claims |

### Academic Terminology
Use pedagogical terms appropriately—our audience knows them:
- ✓ Learning objectives, outcomes, competencies
- ✓ Formative and summative assessment
- ✓ Bloom's taxonomy, cognitive levels
- ✓ Scaffolding, zone of proximal development
- ✓ Active learning, engagement strategies
- ✓ Backward design, constructive alignment

### Phrases to Avoid

| Avoid | Why | Alternative |
|-------|-----|-------------|
| "Game-changing" | Hyperbolic, startup-speak | "A meaningful shift in..." |
| "Unlock your potential" | Generic, patronizing | "Develop approaches that..." |
| "It's that simple!" | Dismissive of complexity | "The process involves..." |
| "You won't believe..." | Clickbait | State the insight directly |
| "Revolutionary AI" | Overpromising | "Research-informed assistance" |
| "Best-in-class" | Empty superlative | Describe specific capabilities |
| "Seamlessly" | Overused, often false | "Integrates with..." |

### Sentence Structure
- **Vary sentence length** for rhythm: mix short declarative sentences with longer explanatory ones
- **Lead with the insight**, not the feature: "Spaced repetition strengthens long-term retention" before "Our Spaced Repetition tool..."
- **Use active voice** predominantly, passive voice for appropriate emphasis
- **Avoid stacking qualifiers**: "very unique innovative approach" → "distinctive approach"

## Content Principles

### 1. Cite, Don't Claim
When making assertions about effectiveness, reference research:
> ✓ "Research on retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006) shows that..."
> ✗ "Studies prove that our method works..."

### 2. Respect Instructor Autonomy
Position tools as aids to decision-making, not replacements for judgment:
> ✓ "Consider these question types for assessing analytical thinking..."
> ✗ "Use these question types for best results..."

### 3. Acknowledge Complexity
Teaching is nuanced. Avoid oversimplification:
> ✓ "While cognitive load theory suggests limiting new information per segment, disciplinary context and student preparation also matter."
> ✗ "Always limit to 3-4 new concepts per class."

### 4. Show, Don't Just Tell
Demonstrate value through specifics:
> ✓ "The tool suggests breaking your 50-minute lecture into four segments with embedded reflection prompts..."
> ✗ "The tool makes your lectures better..."

### 5. Be Honest About Limitations
AI-generated content is a starting point:
> ✓ "Generated learning objectives provide a foundation you'll want to refine based on your specific context and student population."
> ✗ "Get perfect learning objectives instantly."

## Editorial Standards

### Capitalization
- **Product Name**: Teaching Assistant (both words capitalized)
- **Feature Names**: Capitalize as proper nouns when referring to specific tools (e.g., "the Spaced Repetition Scheduler")
- **Pedagogical Terms**: Lowercase unless starting a sentence (e.g., "cognitive load theory," "Bloom's taxonomy")

### Numbers
- Spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10+
- Use numerals for statistics and data: "74% of students..."
- Use numerals with units: "5 learning objectives," "3 modules"

### Punctuation
- **Em dashes**: Use for emphasis or interruption—like this
- **Oxford comma**: Always (clarity, consistency, and professionalism)
- **Exclamation points**: Avoid entirely in product copy; use sparingly elsewhere

### Abbreviations
- Spell out on first use: "Learning Management System (LMS)"
- Common academic abbreviations may be used without expansion: "GPA," "TA," "PhD"

## Brand Voice Checklist

Before publishing any content, ask:

- [ ] Does this sound like a knowledgeable colleague, not a salesperson?
- [ ] Have we avoided startup buzzwords and hyperbole?
- [ ] Is the language accessible without being condescending?
- [ ] Do claims connect to research or evidence?
- [ ] Does the tone respect instructor expertise and autonomy?
- [ ] Is every word necessary?
- [ ] Would this feel at home in a university context?
- [ ] Does the visual-verbal pairing feel cohesive?
