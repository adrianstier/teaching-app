# Persona-Driven Evaluation Report

## Evaluation Methodology
Selected 10 representative personas across different roles, disciplines, tech proficiencies, and skepticism levels to evaluate the Teaching Assistant app. Each persona evaluates the app based on their specific context, goals, and concerns.

---

## Persona Evaluations

### Persona 1: Dr. Sarah Chen (persona-001)
**Profile:** Associate Professor, Molecular Biology, R1 University, 12 years teaching, 180 students
**Tech:** Intermediate, MacBook/Safari, High time pressure (grant deadlines)
**Goals:** Reduce cognitive overload, better formative assessments
**Skepticism:** Cautiously optimistic about edtech, skeptical about AI, high evidence threshold

#### What Works Well
- Research citations on each feature page build credibility
- "Why" explanations for each tool align with evidence-based approach
- Cognitive Load Analyzer directly addresses her primary goal
- Clean, professional aesthetic feels appropriate for academic tool

#### Issues Identified
1. **CRITICAL - No file upload for slides** - She wants to "upload existing slides" but there's no visible upload workflow for the Cognitive Load feature
2. **HIGH - Quiz generation flow unclear** - "Generate quiz questions" journey requires too many clicks to understand
3. **MEDIUM - Research citations collapse state** - Would prefer citations always visible (high evidence threshold)
4. **MEDIUM - No bulk operations** - 180 students means she needs efficiency, no batch processing visible

---

### Persona 2: Marcus Williams (persona-002)
**Profile:** Adjunct Lecturer, English Composition, Community College, 4 years teaching, 28 students
**Tech:** Basic, older Windows laptop/Chrome, Extreme time pressure (5 sections, 2 campuses)
**Goals:** Time-efficient feedback, help struggling writers without burnout
**Skepticism:** Weary of edtech, worried about AI/academic integrity, moderate evidence threshold

#### What Works Well
- Plain language descriptions ("What Actually Sticks" vs jargon)
- "Best for" labels help quick tool selection
- Growth Mindset Feedback tool addresses his feedback needs

#### Issues Identified
1. **CRITICAL - No visible time-saving metrics** - He'll abandon without clear "saves X minutes" promises
2. **CRITICAL - No mention of cost/pricing** - "Can I afford this?" is top concern
3. **HIGH - Academic integrity not addressed** - No mention of how AI-generated content relates to plagiarism concerns
4. **HIGH - Navigation hidden on mobile** - Extreme time pressure means he's likely on phone between campuses
5. **MEDIUM - No "quick start" for common tasks** - Needs "I have 10 minutes before class" workflows

### Persona 3: Professor Eleanor Hartwell (persona-003)
**Profile:** Full Professor, Philosophy, Liberal Arts College, 28 years teaching, 18 students
**Tech:** Low proficiency, iMac/Firefox, Prefers large text, Low time pressure (sabbatical)
**Goals:** Deepen Socratic discussion, help students transfer philosophical reasoning
**Skepticism:** Deeply skeptical of edtech, philosophically concerned about AI, very high evidence threshold

#### What Works Well
- Serif typography feels scholarly, not startup-y
- Case-Based Learning tool directly relevant to Socratic method
- Research basis citations available for scrutiny
- Calm, unhurried aesthetic matches her low time pressure

#### Issues Identified
1. **CRITICAL - STEM bias in examples** - "Photosynthesis" examples throughout, no humanities examples visible
2. **CRITICAL - No accessibility controls** - Prefers large text but no visible font size controls
3. **HIGH - Too many features presented at once** - Overwhelming for low-tech user
4. **HIGH - Navigation dropdowns may be confusing** - Complex hover/click interactions
5. **MEDIUM - Philosophical depth unclear** - "Can AI understand dialectical thinking?" - no evidence this is addressed
